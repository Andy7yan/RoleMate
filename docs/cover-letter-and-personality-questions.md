## Cover letter rules

### Length

- Normally one page and approximately **300–450 words**.
- Graduate and junior applications should normally remain below **500 words**.
- Recruiters are likely to scan rather than read every sentence closely.

### Recommended structure

1. **Opening — exact role and company reason (50–70 words)**
   - Name the exact role.
   - Identify a real product, technical direction, customer problem or mission that matters to Andy.
   - Avoid an empty opening such as `I am excited to apply`.

2. **Core fit — evidence for the most important requirements (180–250 words)**
   - Select the two or three most important requirements from the job description.
   - Prove the match using projects, research, employment or team experience.
   - Explain the problem and work performed rather than listing the resume technology stack again.

3. **Company and team fit (70–100 words)**
   - Connect Andy's direction to the company's business, technology, customers or values.
   - Explain the kind of work he wants to perform in the exact team.
   - Avoid generic praise that could be sent to another employer.

4. **Close (30–50 words)**
   - Briefly summarise fit and express interest in further discussion.
   - Include availability or work-right information only when it materially affects the application.

### Core principle

A cover letter is not a prose copy of the resume. It explains **why the selected evidence is sufficient to show that Andy fits this exact position**. All claims must come from verified experience; never invent an example to match the job description.

### Lessons from the approved Nuix version

- The opening joins education, broad experience and the exact Nuix products without excessive praise.
- WorkTrace AI is used to prove both cross-stack implementation and business-to-engineering translation.
- Research is included only for the distinct value it adds: experimentation, statistical analysis and rigorous AI evaluation.
- The letter presents Andy as an early-career engineer who can contribute now while developing further, rather than overstating seniority.
- The final role motivation is concrete: Java, Python, React/TypeScript and trustworthy insights from complex data.
- A values answer and a cover letter may share the same motivation, but they should not repeat the same paragraph. The cover letter must provide additional evidence of role fit.

## Personality and apparently unrelated questions

Examples include:

- What are your interests or hobbies?
- Tell us something not on your resume.
- What is your superpower?
- What would your teammates say about you?
- What energises you outside work?

These questions are not primarily technical tests. They may assess authenticity, self-awareness, communication style, cultural fit, confidence, humility and whether the applicant would be pleasant and interesting to work with.

### Required workflow

Do not generate a universal personality answer. For each question:

1. Identify the exact wording and company context.
2. Explain what trait or risk the question is likely testing.
3. Ask Andy for the true hobby, behaviour or working strength he wants represented.
4. Develop it into a concise answer with one concrete detail or example.
5. Preserve personality; do not convert every hobby into a disguised software-engineering achievement.

## Hobby and interest questions

### What a good answer should do

- Name one or two real interests rather than producing a long list.
- Explain what Andy actually enjoys about them.
- Include a small concrete detail, routine or recent example so the answer sounds lived rather than manufactured.
- Reveal something useful about personality: curiosity, patience, creativity, sociability, discipline or willingness to explore.
- Remain safe and workplace-appropriate.

### What to avoid

- Inventing a fashionable hobby.
- Claiming every hobby improves productivity or leadership.
- Giving only generic nouns such as `reading, travelling and music` without any detail.
- Making the answer so polished that it no longer sounds personal.
- Choosing a controversial topic unless the context clearly supports it.

## “What is your superpower?”

### Interpretation

This asks for a **distinctive, repeatable strength**, not a literal extraordinary ability. The interviewer may be testing whether Andy:

- understands his own working style;
- can state a strength confidently without arrogance;
- supports it with evidence;
- understands how that strength benefits a team or role;
- avoids empty claims such as `I work hard`, `I am a perfectionist` or `I never give up`.

### Recommended structure

1. Name the strength in one clear phrase.
2. Explain how it appears in practice.
3. Give one brief verified example.
4. State the benefit to the team or work.
5. Keep it around 60–100 words unless another limit is provided.

### Strong candidate direction for Andy

A credible recurring strength in Andy's verified experience is:

> **Turning ambiguous, complex problems into structured and testable work.**

Evidence that may support this direction when appropriate:

- translating an external client's business needs into scoped engineering work for WorkTrace AI;
- coordinating work across a browser extension, desktop application, web app and backend;
- converting an open research question into a multi-stage GPU experiment and statistical analysis pipeline;
- detecting confounds and revising the methodology rather than accepting the first apparent result.

This is only an evidence-backed candidate direction. Before drafting a final answer, confirm that Andy wants this trait to represent him and tailor it to the exact company and role.

### Other possible directions

Use only when supported by the specific content Andy provides:

- connecting technical implementation with business purpose;
- learning unfamiliar systems quickly and building a coherent mental model;
- noticing assumptions, edge cases or methodological weaknesses others may overlook;
- coordinating people and components around a shared outcome.

Do not use more than one central “superpower” in the final answer.
